About

Portland, OR. Usually near coffee.

I've spent about six years in electrical engineering, mostly on hardware: PCB design, prototyping, and board bring-up. I work at Continuous Solutions, a Portland company that develops and tests electric motors and power systems. The company runs on SBIR and STTR contracts, some of them with university and national-lab partners, including Purdue.

The job has pulled me in two directions. One is project management: I'm the project manager for our engineering team, which in practice means assigning and tracking tasks, running weekly check-ins and monthly risk reporting, and raising problems early with a proposed fix attached. The other is infrastructure: our contracts require CMMC Level 2 preparedness, so I keep the engineering intranet running on NGINX, Gitea, and Baserow and manage the Docker host and the FortiGate firewall. I also wrote the company's AI usage SOP.

The part I like is the build: layout, assembly, bring-up, and test. Every project on this page went through that at my bench.

Away from the bench I'm usually 3D printing, doing yard work, or hiking with my dogs, who are the reason the treat dispenser exists.

RF hardwareFeatured project

RF amplifier

The goal was an amplifier I could hold to numbers: hit the gain and bandwidth targets, keep both ports matched to 50 ohms, and keep the noise figure low. The design was simulated in ADS and SPICE before any copper was ordered.

Layout followed RF practice, with short return paths and placement driven by the signal chain. I assembled the boards by hand, SMD and through-hole, then measured S-parameters on a Keysight PNA-X and checked spectral behavior on a spectrum analyzer. The characterization report records where the board met its targets and where it missed them.

RF amplifier specifications
Design and simulationADS, SPICE
LayoutKiCad, RF best practices, 50 ohm matching
AssemblyHand-soldered SMD and through-hole
VerificationVNA S-parameters, spectrum analyzer, full characterization report

Flexible PCB design study

Flex design follows different rules than rigid board work, so this project was a structured pass through them: polyimide substrate selection, adhesiveless stackups, bend radius limits, and where stiffeners and coverlay belong.

Manufacturing drove as much of the design as the electrical requirements. Flex fabs enforce their own DFM rules, so the layout had to meet those as well. Finished panels were checked electrically, with VNA measurements confirming behavior.

Flexible PCB design study specifications
SubstrateAdhesiveless polyimide (Kapton)
MechanicalBend radius compliance, dynamic and static flex
DetailingStiffeners, coverlay, flex-to-rigid transitions
VerificationVNA characterization, fab DFM review

Power and motionSenior capstone

Electric bike retrofit

My senior capstone: convert a regular bicycle into a dependable electric-assist ride. That meant sizing a BLDC hub motor, pairing it with a VESC controller, and building a lithium-ion pack with a battery management system to keep charge and discharge inside safe limits.

Throttle and pedal-assist inputs feed the controller, and regenerative braking recovers energy on descents. Most of the work was integration: mounting, wiring, and tuning across repeated road tests.

Electric bike retrofit specifications
DriveBLDC hub motor
ControlVESC motor controller, throttle and pedal assist
EnergyLi-ion pack with BMS, regenerative braking
StatusSenior capstone, road tested and tuned
